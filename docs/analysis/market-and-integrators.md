# Dustin — Market, Integrators, Positioning and Market Risks

Status: research memo for the Dustin Instaward (30 days, classic G accounts, testnet only). Written 2026-09-25. Figures are dated inline and labelled **evidence** (read from a primary source) or **estimate** (derived here with a stated assumption). Sources `[S#]` and assumptions follow at the end. The competitive facts referenced here are established in the companion memo `competitive-landscape.md`.

## 1. Summary

1. **The problem is structural and large in count, small in value per account.** Stellar holds 10,984,809 accounts and 43,656,439 trustlines (2026-09-24, evidence); 9,552,157 of those trustlines (21.9%) carry no balance yet each locks a 0.5 XLM reserve — about 4.78 million XLM, roughly $1.04 million at $0.2185/XLM (estimate). Only 75,779 accounts (0.69%) were active on 2026-09-24, against a StellarExpert estimate of ~4% daily-active in 2019 (indicative). [S1][S2][S3]
2. **Demand for closure is proven, not hypothetical.** The StellarExpert Demolisher's mediator has forwarded 58,388 payments since 2019 (≈ one per closed account) and still receives about 81 merges a month (Aug 2025–Sep 2026), LOBSTR built and charges for its own custodial merge tool, XLMwallet and xBull ship merge affordances, the SDF ran an SCF RFP for a demolisher in 2026, funded two responses and drew at least two more unfunded ones, and a bootcamp wallet asked for a `mergeAccount` helper on 2026-09-24. [S4][S5][S6][S7][S8][S30]
3. **The integrators with the acute version of the problem are those that *create* accounts for others**: embedded-wallet and onboarding SDKs, anchors and on/off-ramps that create accounts and trustlines during SEP-24 or SDP registration, wallets offering "free account activation" (i.e. sponsored reserves), custodians consolidating dormant sub-accounts, and airdrop/bootcamp/quest programmes. Their stuck reserves sit in accounts that have **zero spendable XLM**, which is exactly the case no shipped tool closes without a hosted service. [S9]–[S14]
4. **Why a library, not a hosted tool**: an integrator's closing flow runs inside its own back end with its own sponsor key; it cannot paste user keys into a web form, cannot depend on a third party's API key, fee cap and mediator, and needs a plan it can log, review and test in CI. The only published programmatic option today, `@lumenwipe/sdk`, is an HTTP client to a hosted API that builds every transaction server side; an unfunded, single-commit planner SDK (scf-account-demolisher, https://github.com/bleu/scf-account-demolisher, unpublished) can produce a dry-run plan in-process but cannot sign, submit or sponsor anything. [S15][S30]
5. **Main market risk**: LumenWipe already has hosted fee sponsorship in code (merged 2026-09-08) and a published SDK; if it switches sponsorship on and drops the API key, Dustin's "first to close zero-XLM accounts" framing is gone. Dustin's defensible claim is therefore **"the backend-free library and CLI that does it, with the integrator's own sponsor key, a read-only planner and a published test matrix"** — section 6 lists the risks and mitigations.

### 2.2 Derived figures (estimates, assumptions stated)

- **XLM locked in empty trustlines**: 9,552,157 × 0.5 XLM = **4,776,079 XLM ≈ $1.04 million**. Assumes StellarExpert's `funded_trustlines` means trustlines with a non-zero balance and that each trustline reserves exactly 0.5 XLM (pool-share trustlines reserve 1 XLM, which makes this a floor).
- **Floor of XLM held as base reserves across all accounts**: 10,984,809 × 1 XLM = **10.98 million XLM ≈ $2.40 million**. All trustline reserves: 43,656,439 × 0.5 = 21.83 million XLM ≈ $4.77 million. Together about 32.8 million XLM ≈ $7.17 million of protocol-required reserve, of which only the dormant share is recoverable value; that share is not published anywhere found here.
- **Dormant share indicator**: 0.69% daily-active today versus "~4% of all Stellar accounts are active" in StellarExpert's 2019 analysis (same operator, methodology described in the 2019 post; the current API field's exact definition is not documented). Read as "the inactive majority has grown", not as a dormant count. [S1][S6]
- **Value per closed account**: an account with 1 XLM base reserve and three empty trustlines frees 2.5 XLM ≈ $0.55. Individually negligible; the business case is operational (support load, ledger hygiene, offboarding, sponsor float) and batch-level, not per-user savings.
- **Sponsor float example**: a wallet that sponsors account creation (2 base reserves) plus one trustline per user carries 1.5 XLM per user; 10,000 churned users lock 15,000 XLM ≈ $3,300 that the sponsor cannot reclaim unilaterally (revoking sponsorship fails when the sponsored account cannot cover the reserve itself), so it needs the user's signature plus a fee-bumped close. [S16][S17]
- **Not found in these sources**: any published count of dormant, zombie or minimum-balance-only accounts. It is computable from Hubble (BigQuery `accounts` table: balance ≤ minimum and last-modified ledger older than N months) and would be a strong addition to the write-up. [S18]

[S29] | A wallet cannot send users to a third-party form with their secret key; it needs `planClose()` output to render a confirmation screen and `executeClose()` to run with its own sponsor key. | **Embedded-wallet and onboarding SDK vendors** | An onboarding/wallet-activation SDK for consumer apps (open source, created 2026-02, active 2026-09) has signed a letter of intent to integrate LumenWipe "into its account closure path" — a vendor treating closure as a required SDK feature. | Such SDKs already run server-side transaction building; they want closure as a dependency in their own process, not a second hosted API with its own key and fee. [S8][S23][S24][S25] |

## 4. Positioning

**For integrators (one paragraph).** Dustin is a TypeScript library and CLI that turns "close this Stellar account" into two calls: `planClose()` inspects any classic account and returns an ordered, read-only plan — which offers to cancel, how each leftover balance will be disposed of (path payment, return to issuer, transfer to the destination, or reported as unclosable with a reason), which trustlines and data entries to remove, what the sponsored entries will return to their sponsor, how much XLM comes back, and the fee of every step; `executeClose()` runs that plan with every transaction wrapped in a fee-bump signed by your own sponsor key, so an account with zero spendable XLM closes without ever paying a fee. It runs in your process on `@stellar/stellar-sdk`: no hosted API, no API key, no third-party mediator or co-signer, and a published edge-case test matrix you can run against the fixture account yourself.

**For SCF reviewers (one paragraph).** The SCF #44 "Account Demolisher" RFP funded two production web tools that cover classic and Soroban DeFi closure through hosted services; the StellarExpert Demolisher has served ~58,000 closures since 2019 but cannot serve an account with zero spendable XLM, and the wallet-SDK helper request (#98) has been open since 2019. Dustin fills the remaining layer under all of them: a backend-free, classic-only library and CLI whose success metric is binary and public — a testnet fixture holding zero spendable XLM, three funded trustlines, an open offer and a data entry is closed with every transaction fee-bumped, and the account no longer exists on a public explorer — delivered with a read-only planner, explicit sponsored-reserve and sequence-number handling, a test matrix, and a recorded baseline of the incumbent stopping on the same account. It is scoped to be reused by the funded tools and by the SDKs, not to replace them.

**Three verifiable differentiators (relative to LumenWipe, the closest competitor).**

1. **Backend-free library with a read-only planner.** Dustin's planner and executor run in-process with no API key, hosted API, mediator account or co-signing server; LumenWipe's SDK is an HTTP client to `api.lumenwipe.com` that "builds no transactions itself and has no `@stellar/stellar-sdk` dependency". The unfunded scf-account-demolisher planner is backend-free too, but it plans only; Dustin pairs the planner with a fee-bumped executor. Verify by reading Dustin's dependency list and running `planClose()` offline against a fixture snapshot. [S15][S30]
2. **Fee-bumped zero-XLM close with the integrator's own sponsor key and no third-party fee cap.** LumenWipe's sponsorship is a hosted endpoint capped at 0.1 XLM per transaction, gated by an API key and listed "Planned" in its own roadmap; the other SCF awardee rejects fee-bump envelopes; the incumbent refuses payouts under 1 XLM. Verify on the explorer: every transaction in Dustin's evidence chain is a fee-bump envelope whose fee source is the sponsor, and the closed account paid nothing. [S15][S26]
3. **Sponsored-trustline unwinding, the `ACCOUNT_MERGE_SEQNUM_TOO_FAR` guard, a published edge-case matrix and a recorded baseline.** Neither funded tool guards the sequence-number case (LumenWipe maps the error to "Please retry"); neither publishes a comparison against the incumbent; the second awardee blocks accounts with foreign sponsorships. Verify by running Dustin's test matrix and watching the baseline recording. [S15][S26]

## 5. Integration surfaces worth naming in the README

- **Wallet back ends**: `planClose()` → confirmation screen; `executeClose()` with a server-held sponsor key; the user signs the inner transactions with the wallet's existing signer.
- **Anchors / SDP operators**: batch CLI over a list of accounts, dry-run by default, JSON plan output for audit logs.
- **Sponsors**: report of reserves returning to the sponsor per account, so the float recovery can be reconciled.
- **Other tooling**: pure planner functions over `@stellar/stellar-sdk` types, so the engine can be embedded by the SCF awardees, by the Wallet SDK (issue #98) or by a future `stellar tx` fee-bump command.

## 6. Market risks and mitigations

| # | Risk | Likelihood / evidence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **LumenWipe ships sponsored fees before Dustin.** Its fee-bump endpoint was merged 2026-09-08 and is on `main`; its SDK is on npm; it has an integration LOI. If it enables the fee account in production and relaxes the API key or cap, Dustin's zero-XLM claim stops being unique. | High; code exists, only deployment status is unknown. [S15][S26] | Do not claim "first to sponsor fees". Claim, and prove, the backend-free library + CLI with the integrator's own sponsor key, no cap, no key, plus the planner and matrix. Ship inside the 30 days. Publish a reproducible comparison: run the same fixture through the incumbent (recorded) and, if their endpoint is enabled, through LumenWipe's hosted flow, and state the results honestly. Target the segments a hosted model serves poorly: batch operators, sponsors reclaiming their own float, air-gapped or self-custody back ends. Keep the engine embeddable so LumenWipe becoming a *user* of Dustin is a possible outcome. |
| R2 | **Duplicate-work perception.** The SCF already funded two demolishers; an Instaward on the same topic can look redundant to the chapter lead or to a future SCF panel. | Medium; the SOW predates awareness of the awards by days. [S27] | State the scope difference explicitly in the README and write-up (classic only, library/CLI, zero-XLM, testnet), link both awardees, credit the unfunded scf-account-demolisher planner as prior art for D1 and make clear that Dustin's contribution over it is D2/D3, and frame Dustin as the reusable engine underneath. If the builder later applies to SCF, consider an integration-style framing with one of the awardees or the Wallet SDK rather than a competing product. |
| R3 | **SDF adds a closure helper to the SDKs or stellar-cli.** | Low near term: issue #98 has had no response in seven years; SDF chose the RFP route in 2026; stellar-cli still lacks a fee-bump wrapper. [S8][S28] | Design Dustin's core as pure functions over `@stellar/stellar-sdk` types with no I/O coupling, so it can be offered upstream as the reference implementation instead of being displaced. |
| R4 | **Fee-bump sponsor abuse.** A sponsor endpoint or CLI can be tricked into paying fees for unrelated transactions, or drained by many small closes. | Real; LumenWipe's threat model documents the same exposure. [S15] | Even though the scope is testnet with an env-key sponsor, implement the same guards now: sponsor only envelopes whose every operation is a wind-down shape (trustline limit 0, offer amount 0, data delete, path payment, merge), inner fee 0, one source account, per-transaction fee cap, per-account and per-minute limits, small sponsor float, and log every sponsored hash. Document the guard list in the write-up. |
| R5 | **Low value per account weakens the pitch.** A typical close frees 1–5 XLM (about $0.22–$1.10 at $0.2185). | Certain. [S3] | Message operational value and batch economics (support tickets, offboarding, sponsor float, ledger hygiene), not user savings. Show the sponsor-float arithmetic in the README. |
| R6 | **Retail demand for the incumbent is declining** (monthly merges into the Demolisher mediator fell from 154 in 2026-01 to 30 in the first 25 days of 2026-09). | Evidence-based; cause unknown (two new tools launched mid-2026). [S5] | Sell to integrators and operators (B2B, batch) rather than retail; retail is already served by three web tools. |
| R7 | **CEX destination expectations.** Both funded tools route exchange payouts through a mediator because exchanges reject `ACCOUNT_MERGE`; Dustin has no mediator by scope. | Certain. [S21][S22] | Document that the destination must be a G account that accepts merges (the integrator's own treasury or the user's other wallet); report an exchange address as unsupported in `planClose()` rather than failing at merge time. |
| R8 | **Testnet-only credibility.** Reviewers may discount results that never touched mainnet. | Medium. | Make the fixture reproducible from a script, publish transaction hashes and the recording, and state the mainnet follow-on (key management, mediator decision) as the next Instaward or SCF step. |
| R9 | **Protocol changes to reserves** (a base-reserve cut would shrink the locked value). | Not found in these sources; watch item only. | None needed now; note it in the write-up's "known limits". |

## Assumptions

1. `funded_trustlines` in the StellarExpert ledger statistics is read as "trustlines with a non-zero balance", and every trustline is costed at 0.5 XLM; pool-share trustlines cost 1 XLM, so the empty-trustline reserve figure is a floor. The field definitions are not documented in these sources.
2. `active_accounts` in the same API is assumed to follow the StellarExpert methodology described in its 2019 analysis (accounts that originated operations, excluding passive destinations); the 2019 "~4%" and the 2026 "0.69%" are compared only as an indication of direction.
3. Dollar conversions use the CoinGecko XLM/USD price on 2026-09-25 ($0.218517) and are illustrative; they are not part of any deliverable.
4. "One mediator payment ≈ one closed account" for the StellarExpert Demolisher assumes the source's one-forward-payment-per-merge design holds for all 58,388 payments; refunds or manual payments would inflate the count slightly.
5. Segment claims (wallets, anchors, sponsors, exchanges, programmes) are supported by the cited documents and directory counts; no integrator has been interviewed, and no letter of intent exists for Dustin. The Pollar letter of intent is LumenWipe's, cited as evidence of demand, not as a Dustin prospect.
6. Award amounts for the SCF #44 awardees are quoted only side by side because three public figures disagree (SCF recap and submission pages: "$120,000 worth of XLM" each; LumenLoop `scf.awarded_total`: 36,000 and 72,000; the Account Demolisher tranche list: 108,000); none is treated as final.
7. Third-party URLs are reproduced verbatim in the Sources section even where a URL contains a publisher's account handle, because every claim must be traceable; no individual is named in the body.
8. Risk likelihoods are the analyst's judgement from the cited evidence, not measured probabilities.
